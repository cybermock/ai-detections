# AI-009 - HDSI AI First Seen Provider For User

> **Detection ID:** AI-009 (`detection_id=hdsi_ai_first_seen_provider_for_user`)
> **Search name:** `Network - HDSI AI First Seen Provider For User - Rule`
> **Package:** `saved_searches/packages/hdsi_ai_first_seen_provider_for_user` (version 1.1.0, `deployment_type: global`, ships `enable_alerting: false`)
> **Status:** Active, risk only (`action.notable = 0`, `action.risk = 1`). Notables come from the `HDSI RBA AI` aggregators.
> **Risk Score:** user only, 5 to 40 (computed `risk_score`; the literal `_risk` fallback is 20). Blocked-only attempts score at half weight.
> **MITRE ATT&CK:** T1588.007 - Obtain Capabilities: Artificial Intelligence (Resource Development)
> **Data Model:** `Web` and/or `Network_Traffic` (either one, accelerated, with a populated `user`)
> **Schedule:** `17 2 * * *` (daily at 02:17)
> **Lookback Window:** `-1d@d` to `@d` (yesterday), with a history window of `-31d@d` to the window start
> **Suppression:** `user,provider` for 86400 s (24 h)
> **Kill Chain Phase:** Reconnaissance (`ai_kill_chain_phase=recon`)
> **Last Updated:** 2026-10-09

---

## Overview

This detection finds a user who starts using a **rarely used AI provider**. Two conditions must hold: the user has no use of that provider in the previous 30 days of accelerated history, and the provider was used by at most 10 people in the organisation that day. Mainstream tools (ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot) produce hundreds of "first uses" a day in a large tenant, so the rarity gate removes them. What remains is a short daily list of people adopting, or trying to reach, niche or unvetted AI services: voice cloning, transcription and meeting bots, code agents, model routers, document AI.

The business risk is data going to a vendor that has had no security or privacy review. A first use is an early signal, before uploads or integrations become routine. Each finding says whether the traffic was `allowed` or `all blocked` by the proxy or NGFW.

The rule writes risk on the user only, never on a system, and creates no notable. It feeds the user threshold aggregators (AI-RISK-001 / AI-RISK-002) and the multi-vector aggregator (`shadow_usage` vector).

Before 1.1.0 the rule grouped by `Web.url_domain` and `Web.site` without `fillnull_value`, so rows with either field empty were dropped. It returned nothing on two validation tenants and wrote risk on the user `unknown` from inbound scanner traffic on a third.

## Detection Logic

### Plain English

1. **Window (yesterday).** Two `tstats` searches read AI traffic only. Web is pre-filtered by `hdsi_ai_first_seen_provider_for_user_web_scope` (AI hostnames in `url_domain` or `site`, plus SSE/CASB app names in `site`). Network_Traffic is pre-filtered to AI App-IDs by `hdsi_ai_first_seen_provider_for_user_nt_scope`. An App-ID with more than 10 users in the day is collapsed to one aggregate row.
2. **Resolve the user.** Unattributed users (empty, `unknown`, `-`, IP addresses, machine accounts ending in `$`) are dropped. The user is looked up in `identity_lookup_expanded`, so `DOMAIN\jsmith`, `jsmith` and `john.smith@example.com` of one person count as one person. `account,location` values from some cloud proxies are reduced to the account.
3. **Resolve the provider** with `ai_domains_filter`: hostnames as `ai_input_type=host`, SSE app names and App-IDs as `ai_input_type=app`. Providers in `..._sanctioned_providers` are dropped.
4. **Candidates.** Providers with at most `max_org_users` (10) distinct people that day.
5. **History.** One `tstats` over the 30 days before the window, restricted to the candidate users (with every identity alias) and the candidate providers' domains, app names and App-IDs.
6. **Decision.** A user/provider pair is reported when it has no overlapping history, the provider passed the rarity gate, and the user made at least `min_events` (3) window events, not counting hits on incidental subdomains (`share.`, `cdn.`, `files.`, `try.`, `docs.`, `help.` ...).
7. **Outcome.** `access_outcome=allowed` when at least one event was not blocked, `all blocked` when every event matched `..._blocked_actions`.
8. Approved users, systems and business units are removed by `ai_unsanctioned_filter(user,src,provider)`, then the finding is scored.

### Scoring

- `risk_score = min(40, max(5, round(20 x severity_weight x risk_multiplier x outcome_weight)))`
- `severity_weight`: from the provider lookup, 0.3 to 1.2.
- `risk_multiplier`: from `ai_risk_multiplier` (identity `bunit` sensitivity, x1.25 for privileged or critical identities, capped at 2.0).
- `outcome_weight`: 1 for `allowed`, `..._blocked_weight` (0.5) for `all blocked`.
- Validation scores ranged 5 to 24.

## Triage Steps

> **Estimated triage time:** 10 to 15 minutes.

### Step 1: Validate the risk event

Set the time picker to the last 7 days. The risk event carries `user_terms`; copy its value for the later steps.

```spl
| from datamodel:"Risk.All_Risk"
| search source="Network - HDSI AI First Seen Provider For User - Rule" risk_object="$user$"
| table _time, risk_object, risk_score, provider, usage_type, access_outcome, window_events, provider_users, ai_domain_list, data_source_list, baseline_days, user_terms, risk_message
| sort - _time
```

- For a `DOMAIN\user` risk object, double the backslash in the search (`risk_object="EXAMPLE\\jsmith"`); a single backslash matches nothing.
- `access_outcome="all blocked"`: policy already stopped the request. Treat it as an attempt and check whether it was repeated or followed by use of another provider.
- `baseline_days` well below 30 means the data model acceleration is shorter than 30 days and "first use" is less reliable.

### Step 2: Identify the user

```spl
| inputlookup identity_lookup_expanded where identity="$user$"
| table identity, first, last, email, managedBy, bunit, category, priority, watchlist, startDate, endDate
```

- A recent `startDate` (new hire) or a user returning from leave has no history; every rare provider they touch is a first use.
- Note `bunit` and `category`: the score already includes the identity multiplier, but the role decides how sensitive the use is.
- No identity record: formats of this person may not be merged, and the same person can appear under a second risk object.

### Step 3: Review the window activity

Replace `$user_terms$` with the value from Step 1, set the time picker to the day in `first_seen`. This is the rule's Web drilldown, limited to the reported provider.

```spl
| tstats summariesonly=t allow_old_summaries=t fillnull_value="unknown" count min(_time) as firstTime max(_time) as lastTime values(Web.src) as src values(Web.action) as action values(Web.http_method) as http_method from datamodel=Web where Web.user IN ($user_terms$) (Web.url_domain=* OR Web.site=*) by Web.user Web.url_domain Web.site
| rename Web.* as *
| eval ai_input_type=if(url_domain="unknown", "app", "host"), url_domain=coalesce(nullif(url_domain, "unknown"), site), ai_domain=lower(url_domain)
| `ai_domains_filter`
| search provider="$provider$"
| convert ctime(firstTime) ctime(lastTime)
| table firstTime, lastTime, user, src, provider, usage_type, url_domain, site, action, http_method, count
| sort - count
```

For NGFW App-ID findings (`data_source_list` contains `network_traffic`):

```spl
| tstats summariesonly=t allow_old_summaries=t count min(_time) as firstTime max(_time) as lastTime values(All_Traffic.src) as src values(All_Traffic.dest) as dest values(All_Traffic.action) as action sum(All_Traffic.bytes_out) as bytes_out from datamodel=Network_Traffic where All_Traffic.user IN ($user_terms$) `hdsi_ai_first_seen_provider_for_user_nt_scope` by All_Traffic.user All_Traffic.app
| rename All_Traffic.* as *
| eval ai_domain=app, ai_input_type="app"
| `ai_domains_filter`
| search provider="$provider$"
| convert ctime(firstTime) ctime(lastTime)
| table firstTime, lastTime, user, src, dest, app, provider, action, bytes_out, count
| sort - count
```

- `api.<provider>` hosts or POST-heavy traffic mean the tool was actually used (a browser app or script calling the API), not only browsed.
- Hits that are only on `share.`, `files.` or `try.` subdomains are content consumption (a shared recording or video) and should not have passed; if they did, check `..._incidental_hosts`.

### Step 4: Confirm there was no earlier use

Set the time picker to `-31d@d` to now. A day with use before `first_seen` (for example under another account format that the identity data does not link) makes this a false first use.

```spl
| tstats summariesonly=t allow_old_summaries=t fillnull_value="unknown" count from datamodel=Web where Web.user IN ($user_terms$) `hdsi_ai_first_seen_provider_for_user_web_scope` by Web.user Web.url_domain Web.site _time span=1d
| rename Web.* as *
| eval ai_input_type=if(url_domain="unknown", "app", "host"), ai_domain=lower(coalesce(nullif(url_domain, "unknown"), site))
| `ai_domains_filter`
| search provider="$provider$"
| eval day=strftime(_time, "%Y-%m-%d")
| stats sum(count) as events values(ai_domain) as names values(user) as accounts by day
| sort day
```

- Also try the person's other known account names (from Step 2 `identity`) if `user_terms` holds only one format.

### Step 5: Check related HDSI AI risk on the user

```spl
| from datamodel:"Risk.All_Risk"
| search source="* - HDSI AI * - Rule" risk_object IN ($user_terms$)
| stats sum(risk_score) as total_risk dc(source) as detections values(source) as sources values(provider) as providers by risk_object
| sort - total_risk
```

- An upload detection (AI-007, AI-044) or DLP detection for the same provider after the first use raises the priority.
- The user threshold aggregators alert at 60 (medium, at least 2 detections) and 90 (critical) within 24 hours.

### Step 6: Check approval status

```spl
| inputlookup ai_sanctioned_entities
| eval key_entity=mvindex(split(allow_key, "|"), 0), key_provider=mvindex(split(allow_key, "|"), 1)
| where enabled="1" AND (key_provider=lower("$provider$") OR key_provider="*")
| table entity_type, entity, provider, scope, expires, owner, reason, allow_key
```

- Check whether the user, their `bunit` or the whole organisation (`*`) is approved for this provider.

### Step 7: Make a determination

- **True Positive:** an unvetted provider used (or repeatedly attempted) by a user with no approval, especially with uploads or API use.
- **Benign True Positive:** real first use of a provider that is approved for the user, their team, or under evaluation by procurement.
- **False Positive:** earlier use exists under another account format, the hits are incidental (shared content, link previews), or the user is new and the provider is common in their team.

---

## True Positive Indicators

- The provider handles sensitive input by design: voice cloning (ElevenLabs, Resemble AI), meeting transcription bots (Otter.ai, Fireflies.ai), code agents and app builders, model routers (OpenRouter), document AI.
- `usage_type=api`, or `api.<provider>` hosts in `ai_domain_list`: a script or integration, not only a visit.
- `access_outcome=allowed` with many events and POST requests.
- The user is in a sensitive business unit, privileged, or on a watchlist.
- Upload, DLP or copy-paste detections for the same user follow within days.
- Repeated `all blocked` attempts followed by use of a different, unblocked provider (shopping around a block).

---

## False Positive Scenarios

Evidence from read-only tests on 4 production ES tenants (2026-10-09; Tenant A 31 and 41 findings on two days, B 1 and 0, C 1 and 1, D 0).

| Scenario | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| New hire, returning leaver or newly onboarded proxy: no history, so every rare provider is a first use | Medium | Exclude onboarding cohorts in `hdsi_ai_first_seen_provider_for_user_filter`; expect a burst after a new data source goes live |
| Same person in two unlinked formats (`DOMAIN\jsmith` on the NGFW, `john.smith@...` on the SSE). On Tenant A about two thirds of flagged users had no identity record, and two people were reported twice on one day | Medium | Load both formats into the ES identity data; the rule needs no change |
| Pooled provider labels (`Model Registry` covers huggingface.co, hf.co and modelscope.cn): earlier use of one counts as history for the others | Low | Split such rows in `ai_provider_domains` if the services must be judged separately |
| Blocked-only attempts (8 of 31 and 12 of 41 findings on Tenant A) | Medium | Reported at half weight; set `..._blocked_weight` to 0 to drop them |
| Rarity measured on a quiet day (weekend) | Low | Use the Threshold SPL to set `max_org_users` per tenant |
| Incidental hits (shared avatar video, meeting-recording link, link preview) | Low | `min_events` and `incidental_hosts` remove most; extend `..._incidental_hosts` if needed |
| History shorter than 30 days because acceleration keeps less (one tenant keeps 7 days) | Medium where acceleration is short | Extend acceleration or accept a shorter baseline; `baseline_days` in the event shows it |

## Response Actions

### If confirmed True Positive

1. **Contain:** block the provider at the proxy, SSE or NGFW if it is prohibited. For meeting bots, remove the bot's calendar or meeting-platform integration and revoke its OAuth grant.
2. **Notify:** the user's manager and the AI governance or vendor risk owner; ask whether there is a business need.
3. **Investigate:** use Step 3 and Step 5 to establish what was sent (uploads, API calls, recordings) and whether other detections fired. For API use, look for API keys in code or DLP (AI-033).
4. **Remediate:** route the request through vendor review; offer the approved alternative.
5. **Document:** record the user, provider, outcome and decision in the case.

### If Benign True Positive

1. Approve the user, team or organisation in `ai_sanctioned_entities`:
   ```
   user,jsmith@example.com,ElevenLabs,provider,2027-03-31,security,approved_marketing_pilot,1,jsmith@example.com|elevenlabs
   group,marketing,ElevenLabs,group,2027-03-31,security,approved_marketing_pilot,1,marketing|elevenlabs
   ```
2. For a provider sanctioned for everyone, add it to `hdsi_ai_first_seen_provider_for_user_sanctioned_providers` (quoted, e.g. `"OpenAI","Microsoft Copilot"`).
3. Record the justification in `reason` and set `expires`.

## Tuning Guidance

### Reducing false positives

- Exclude service accounts and inbound sourcetypes in `hdsi_ai_first_seen_provider_for_user_filter` (Web, e.g. `NOT Web.user IN ("svc_*")`, `NOT sourcetype IN ("aws:elb:accesslogs","aws:waf")`) and `hdsi_ai_first_seen_provider_for_user_nt_filter` (NGFW). On a tenant whose Web model is mostly inbound load-balancer and WAF logs (Tenant B), excluding them also removes most of the 5 to 6 minute run time.
- Add vendor-specific block verbs to `hdsi_ai_first_seen_provider_for_user_blocked_actions`.
- Override `risk_score` (at most 100) in `hdsi_ai_first_seen_provider_for_user_risk_score`.

### Adjusting thresholds

Daily distinct people per provider across both sources; choose `max_org_users` below the providers your organisation treats as mainstream. Run over the last 7 days, one day at a time on a very large NGFW tenant.

```spl
| tstats summariesonly=t allow_old_summaries=t fillnull_value="unknown" count from datamodel=Web where earliest=-7d@d latest=@d Web.user=* `hdsi_ai_first_seen_provider_for_user_web_scope` by _time span=1d Web.user Web.url_domain Web.site
| rename Web.* as *
| eval data_source="web"
| append [| tstats summariesonly=t allow_old_summaries=t count from datamodel=Network_Traffic where earliest=-7d@d latest=@d All_Traffic.user=* `hdsi_ai_first_seen_provider_for_user_nt_scope` by _time span=1d All_Traffic.user All_Traffic.app | rename All_Traffic.* as * | eval data_source="network_traffic"]
| `hdsi_ai_first_seen_provider_for_user_resolve`
| stats dc(user_key) as users by _time, provider
| stats median(users) as median_daily_users max(users) as max_daily_users by provider
| sort - median_daily_users
```

Check the acceleration range against `..._baseline`:

```spl
| rest /services/admin/summarization by_tstats=t splunk_server=local count=0
| search summary.id="*Web*" OR summary.id="*Network_Traffic*"
| table summary.id, summary.time_range
```

### Lookup maintenance

The Web and App-ID pre-filters (`..._web_scope`, `..._domains`, `..._apps`, `..._nt_scope`) are literal lists generated from the common lookups. Regenerate them with the SPL in the package README when `ai_provider_domains` or `ai_provider_apps` gains rows. App-IDs missing from `ai_provider_apps` are invisible to the NGFW leg.
